They need the highest probability of finding the optimal hyperparameters within this limited budget.<br><br>Which search strategy should the team use? By setting `EnableEarlyStopping: True` in the `TrainingJobDefinition` (or `AlgorithmSpecification`), SageMaker monitors the objective metric. If a training job is underperforming compared to the median performance of completed jobs at the same step, SageMaker automatically terminates the job, saving compute time and cost.<br><br>**Distractor Analysis:**<br>- **B is Incorrect:** Lowering `MaxTrainingJobs` limits the overall search space and the chance of finding a good model, rather than specifically stopping bad jobs early.<br>- **C is Incorrect:** Random search does not evaluate job performance during the run; it only selects parameters randomly and will run jobs to completion unless early stopping is explicitly enabled.<br>- **D is Incorrect:** Building a custom SageMaker Pipelines step to monitor and kill jobs introduces unnecessary operational overhead and complexity when AMT provides this natively.<br><br>> **Key Takeaway:** Enable automatic early stopping in AMT (`EnableEarlyStopping: True`) to halt underperforming training jobs automatically based on their objective metric trajectory. |

The learning rate is a floating-point number that can take any value between two bounds (0.0001 and 0.01), which requires a `ContinuousParameterRange`.<br><br>**Distractor Analysis:**<br>- **A is Incorrect:** The optimizer is a string categorical, not an integer. | **### Question 10 - Selecting Hyperband for Massive Budgets**<br><br>A research team is training a massive deep learning model for image classification. Each training job is computationally expensive and takes several hours. They have a large budget allowing up to 500 training jobs, but they want to aggressively stop underperforming trials early and allocate more resources to the promising ones.<br><br>Which search strategy is BEST suited for this scenario? (Select ONE)<br><br>- **A)** Bayesian Optimization<br>- **B)** Random Search<br>- **C)** Hyperband<br>- **D)** Grid Search | **Correct Answer:** **C**<br><br>**Correct Option Analysis:**<br>- **C is Correct:** Hyperband is a search strategy specifically designed for large-scale hyperparameter tuning. It uses aggressive early stopping to dynamically allocate resources. It starts by running many configurations for a short time, discards the underperforming ones, and progressively runs the promising ones for longer. This is ideal for computationally expensive jobs where you have a large budget and want to maximize the discovery of top-performing models without wasting hours on bad parameter combinations.<br><br>**Distractor Analysis:**<br>- **A is Incorrect:** While Bayesian optimization is good for limited budgets, it does not natively implement the aggressive, multi-stage early stopping of Hyperband. For 500 expensive jobs, Bayesian might waste significant compute on early epochs of bad models before learning to avoid them.<br>- **B is Incorrect:** Random Search runs jobs blindly without learning from previous trials and does not natively feature the dynamic resource allocation that makes Hyperband efficient for large numbers of expensive jobs.<br>- **D is Incorrect:** Grid Search is not supported in SageMaker AMT and would be exponentially expensive for a 500-job budget across continuous parameters.<br><br>> **Key Takeaway:** Use the `Hyperband` search strategy for massive tuning budgets with computationally expensive jobs, as it aggressively stops bad jobs early and reallocates resources to promising candidates. |
