| **### Question 4 - Stop Word Removal in RAG Pipelines**<br><br>An AI engineering team is building a RAG application using **Amazon OpenSearch Service** as a vector store. The source documents are technical engineering manuals. Removing stop words can fundamentally change the meaning of a sentence (e.g., "The machine is not running" vs. "machine not running").<br><br>**Distractor Analysis:**<br>- **A is Incorrect:** While it reduces token count, the cost savings are negligible compared to the degradation in retrieval quality. Modern models are highly sensitive to missing context.<br>- **C is Incorrect:** OpenSearch stores the resulting numerical vectors, not the raw text. The presence of stop words in the source text prior to embedding has no impact on OpenSearch's ability to index the vectors.<br>- **D is Incorrect:** Although true that stop word removal is a legacy bag-of-words technique, the question asks for the most accurate assessment of this step in the context of the current Bedrock/OpenSearch RAG pipeline, making B the more complete and relevant answer.<br><br>> **Key Takeaway:** Do not apply traditional stop word removal to text before passing it to modern transformer-based embedding models; these models require the full sentence context to generate accurate dense vectors. |
