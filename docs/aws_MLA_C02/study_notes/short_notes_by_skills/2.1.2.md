# Skill 2.1.2: Identify fine-tuning strategies for pre-trained FMs to meet business needs
## Task 2.1: ML Model and Foundation Model (FM) Development - Choose appropriate modeling approaches for ML and AI solutions

### Core Decision: Where Does the Need Live?
| Business Need | Best-Fit Approach | Why / AWS Path |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed logic** (thresholds, reorder rules) | **Rules / code** | No model; cheaper, faster, auditable |
| **Standard pre-defined task** (OCR, speech, vision, NLP) | **Managed AWS AI service** | Textract, Rekognition, Transcribe, Comprehend |
| **Dynamic facts, citations, fresh docs** | **RAG** | Bedrock Knowledge Bases; external vector index; no weight change |
| **Consistent behavior, tone, format, domain jargon** | **Fine-tuning** | Writes behavior into weights; custom model |
| **Both fresh knowledge and behavior** | **RAG + fine-tuning** | RAG supplies facts; fine-tuning supplies style/format |

> **Exam Gotcha:** Fine-tuning is for **behavior, format, tone, and domain adaptation** — not for reliably adding new or frequently changing factual knowledge. Use **RAG** for knowledge freshness and citations.

---

### Fine-Tuning Strategy Matrix
| Strategy | Best For | Data Required | AWS Path | Key Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** | Domain terminology, output format, instruction following, task specialization | **Labeled prompt-response pairs** (JSONL, S3), validation holdout | Bedrock fine-tuning, SageMaker JumpStart | Needs quality data, training cost, versioning; risk of catastrophic forgetting |
| **Continued Pre-Training (CPT)** | Domain adaptation on large unlabeled corpus (legal, medical, financial) | **Unlabeled domain text** | Bedrock continued pre-training, SageMaker | Expensive; can degrade general ability; usually followed by SFT |
| **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning / RLHF** | Aligning to human preferences, safety, complex reasoning, reward optimization | **Preference pairs** (chosen/rejected) or **reward function** | Bedrock RFT, SageMaker RLHF | Complex reward design; costly; needs careful evaluation |
| **Distillation** | Reducing cost/latency of a large teacher model while preserving quality | **Prompts**; teacher generates responses | Bedrock model distillation | Student may lose quality; no human labels needed |
| **PEFT / LoRA / QLoRA** | Limited compute, faster iteration, lower forgetting risk | Labeled data | SageMaker JumpStart, custom training | Adapter weights; may not match full fine-tuning quality |
| **Full Fine-Tuning** | Maximum adaptation when PEFT insufficient | Large labeled dataset | SageMaker AI | Highest cost; highest catastrophic forgetting risk |

> **Order of Operations:** **Prompt engineering → RAG → Fine-tuning.** Fine-tune only when evaluations prove prompting + retrieval cannot close a persistent gap.

### AWS Implementation Paths
| Service | Use When | Customization Options | Inference / Cost Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amazon Bedrock** | Managed FMs, fastest path, no infrastructure | Fine-tuning, continued pre-training, reinforcement fine-tuning, distillation | **Custom models require Provisioned Throughput**; ongoing cost, not per-token |
| **SageMaker JumpStart** | Open-source FMs, PEFT/LoRA/QLoRA, solution templates | Fine-tuning, PEFT, full fine-tuning | Deploy to SageMaker endpoints; more control |
| **SageMaker AI** | Full control, custom containers, distributed training, HPO | Any fine-tuning method, custom scripts, pipelines | Manage endpoints, scaling, spot training, model registry |

### Data & Configuration Essentials
- **SFT data:** labeled prompt-response pairs, JSONL, S3; high quality > large quantity; consistent formatting.
- **CPT data:** unlabeled domain corpus; **mix general data** to reduce catastrophic forgetting.
- **RFT/RLHF data:** preference pairs or reward function; define reward carefully.
- **Distillation data:** prompts only; teacher model generates responses for student training.
- **Hyperparameters:** epochs, batch size, learning rate, warmup, early stopping; monitor validation loss.
- **Evaluation:** compare baseline vs fine-tuned using automatic/human evaluation; task metrics (accuracy, ROUGE, BERTScore, human preference).

> **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Narrow fine-tuning can erase general capabilities. Mitigate with mixed data, PEFT, or lower learning rate/epochs.

---

### Security, Compliance & Cost
- **IAM roles:** Bedrock/SageMaker roles need S3, KMS, and model access permissions.
- **KMS:** encrypt training data, model artifacts, and custom models at rest; TLS in transit.
- **VPC endpoints (PrivateLink):** private access to Bedrock/SageMaker without public internet.
- **Data privacy:** Bedrock does not train base models on customer data; custom models are private to your account.
- **Cost drivers:** training GPU hours + inference. Bedrock custom models = Provisioned Throughput. SageMaker = real-time, serverless, async, or batch endpoints.
- **Latency:** distillation and smaller models reduce latency; PEFT does **not** reduce inference latency unless deployed as a smaller model.

---

### Anti-Patterns & Exam Gotchas
> **Prompt first:** Fine-tuning before prompt engineering is a costly anti-pattern.
> **RAG vs fine-tuning:** RAG = external knowledge, citations, freshness. Fine-tuning = internal behavior, style, format.
> **Continued pre-training uses unlabeled data; SFT uses labeled prompt-response pairs.**
> **Distillation uses teacher-generated responses, not human labels.**
> **Bedrock custom models require Provisioned Throughput for inference** — plan for ongoing cost.
> **Fine-tuning for fixed logic is wrong** — use rules.
> **Always evaluate baseline vs fine-tuned** before production rollout.
